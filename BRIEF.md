# Map of AI — Founding Brief (from Alan Blount, verbatim intent)

The basic idea is a two-dimensional, multi-altitude representation of the
history and the world of AI research. The visualization will be reminiscent
of cartography, and west is old, east is new. There is a furthest point of
old, basically the invention of the domains of artificial intelligence,
potentially going all the way back to Alan Turing and maybe before. On the
east, whatever the current day is, and potentially we would update this map
every month as new domains and significant industries open up. North and
south will be some other axis, and I don't exactly know which axis to do.
Perhaps there are good trends in the industry of AI that could help inform
that. We'd be open to suggestion, and we would try to localize similar
schools of thought with each other.

Your job at this point is to start creating a compendium of research from
authoritative sources. Most of this actually is probably already in
Wikipedia or other useful authoritative sources about anything older than
perhaps a year ago. Within the last year, I'm unsure of what the best source
of information for you would be.

You should draw inspiration from mapofmetal.com, which you can only use from
a browser, and it takes a while for it to load, and the user interface might
not be so great for driving a headless browser because it is an engaging
drag-and-drop Google Maps style interface. Somebody put a lot of love and
care into that website, both the creation of the visual representation of
the cartography of the genre of music and also the curation of the content.

We will likewise try and put some love into visually aesthetically pleasing
map as well as the curation of content, but we need to start with an axis
point.

## Concrete asks
1. Understand the intention above and turn it into a real project plan.
2. Propose theories/candidates for the north-south axis (X axis is fixed:
   west=oldest AI history, east=present day, updated monthly).
3. Plan for 2-4 "altitudes" of zoom detail (rough continent-level layout down
   to fine-grained schools-of-thought / people / papers), analogous to
   mapofmetal.com's zoom levels.
4. Start a compendium of research: pull from authoritative sources
   (Wikipedia, AAAI/ACM/academic histories, etc.) for anything older than
   ~1 year old. For the last 12 months, propose the best sourcing strategy
   (arXiv, industry blogs, conference proceedings, etc.) since this is a
   moving target requiring monthly updates.
5. Try to spatially cluster/localize similar schools of thought near each
   other (this is the cartographic curation work, not just a timeline).

Aesthetic bar: this should feel as lovingly crafted as mapofmetal.com, both
in visual design and in content curation quality.
