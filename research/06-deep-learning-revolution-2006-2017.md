# The Deep Learning Revolution (2006–2017)

> **Status:** verified
> **Date range:** 2006–2017
> **Map placement (proposed):** X = 2006–2017; Y = connectionist pole (the whole continent sits on the connectionist/statistical bank; a narrow "generative modelling" shoreline and a "deep RL" delta reach toward the middle of the map)
> **Altitude:** A1 continent ("The Deep Learning Uplands" / "GPU Highlands"), A2 country (Toronto–Montréal–NYU "Canadian Mafia", Google Brain, DeepMind, Stanford/ImageNet, IDSIA, FAIR, OpenAI), A3 city (AlexNet, ImageNet, word2vec, DQN, GAN, seq2seq/attention, ResNet, TensorFlow, AlphaGo), A4 street (individual tricks: ReLU, dropout, batch norm, Adam; frameworks: Theano, Caffe, Keras, PyTorch)

## One-paragraph summary
Between 2006 and 2017 multi-layer neural networks went from an unfashionable niche to the default method for perception, language and control, and the phrase "deep learning" replaced "neural networks" as the banner of the connectionist pole [1]. The era opens quietly with Hinton, Osindero and Teh's greedy layer-wise pre-training of deep belief nets (2006) and the Hinton–Salakhutdinov autoencoder paper in *Science* [2][3][4]; it acquires its engine when Raina, Madhavan and Ng (2009) and Cireşan, Meier and Schmidhuber at IDSIA (2011–12) show that Nvidia GPUs and CUDA make training large convolutional nets practical, and its fuel when Fei-Fei Li's group releases ImageNet (CVPR 2009) and launches the ILSVRC challenge (2010) [5][6][7][8][12][13]. The decisive event is AlexNet at ILSVRC 2012 — a top-5 error of 15.3% against 26.2% for the runner-up — which, together with the same year's Google Brain "cat" experiment and the deep-nets-for-speech consensus paper, convinced industry to buy the field: Google acquired Hinton's DNNresearch (March 2013) and DeepMind (January 2014), Facebook hired LeCun to found FAIR (December 2013), Baidu hired Ng, and OpenAI was founded (December 2015) [11][15][17][18][20][21][45][59][62]. In the five years that followed, nearly the whole vocabulary of modern deep learning was written: word2vec (2013), DQN (2013/2015), VAEs and GANs (2013–14), seq2seq and Bahdanau attention (2014), VGG/GoogLeNet/ResNet (2014–15), Adam and batch normalisation (2014–15), open frameworks (Theano, Caffe, Keras, TensorFlow, PyTorch), and custom silicon (Google's TPU) [22][25][27][28][30][31][33][34][35][36][42][44][49][52]. AlphaGo's 4–1 defeat of Lee Sedol in March 2016 was the era's Deep-Blue moment and AlphaGo Zero (October 2017) its coda; two months earlier, in June 2017, eight Google researchers had posted "Attention Is All You Need", the Transformer paper that hands the map over to the next continent (file 07) [46][48][54][55]. The founders' achievement was canonised retroactively — the 2018 Turing Award to Bengio, Hinton and LeCun, and the 2024 Nobel Prize in Physics to Hopfield and Hinton [56][57].

## Timeline of key events

|------|-------|----------------|--------|
| 2006 (Jul) | Hinton, Osindero & Teh, "A Fast Learning Algorithm for Deep Belief Nets" (*Neural Computation* 18(7)); Hinton & Salakhutdinov, "Reducing the Dimensionality of Data with Neural Networks" (*Science*, 28 Jul 2006) | Greedy layer-wise pre-training of stacked RBMs makes "deep" networks trainable again; the papers that re-brand neural nets as "deep learning" | [2][3][4] |
| 2007–2009 | Nvidia officially releases CUDA (2007; created 2004); Raina, Madhavan & Ng (Stanford), "Large-scale Deep Unsupervised Learning using Graphics Processors" (ICML 2009) train a 100M-parameter DBN on GTX 280 GPUs up to 70× faster than CPUs | GPUs become general-purpose parallel machines and the first widely cited demonstration that they change what is feasible for deep nets; the Silicon River (file 09d) enters this continent | [5][1][6] |
| 2009 (Jun) | Deng, Dong, Socher, Li, Li & Fei-Fei present ImageNet at CVPR 2009 (poster, Florida); labelling via Amazon Mechanical Turk ran Jul 2008 – Apr 2010, ~14 million images | The dataset that made scale in supervised vision possible | [7][8] |
| 2010 | First ImageNet Large Scale Visual Recognition Challenge (ILSVRC): 1,000 classes; 2010 and 2011 winners are SVM-based feature pipelines (top-5 error ~25% in 2011) | Establishes the benchmark that deep learning will crack two years later | [8][9] |
| 2009–2011 | IDSIA (Schmidhuber's lab): Graves et al.'s LSTM+CTC becomes the first RNN to win a pattern-recognition contest (connected handwriting, 2009); Cireşan, Meier, Masci, Gambardella & Schmidhuber's GPU CNN ("DanNet") achieves the first superhuman result in a visual pattern-recognition contest (IJCNN traffic signs, 2011) | GPU-trained CNNs beat humans before AlexNet — Cireşan et al. win four image contests between May 2011 and Sep 2012; the recurrent branch proves itself on sequences | [1][70][12][13][18] |
| 2011 | Google Brain project begins at Google X (Jeff Dean, Greg Corrado, Andrew Ng); DistBelief distributed-training system built from 2011 (Dean et al., NIPS 2012) | Industrial-scale deep learning infrastructure; predecessor of TensorFlow | [14][16][44] |
| 2012 (Jun) | Le et al., "Building High-level Features Using Large Scale Unsupervised Learning" (ICML 2012; arXiv 1112.6209, 29 Dec 2011) — the Google Brain "cat" network trained on 10 million YouTube frames on 16,000 cores | Unsupervised feature learning at unprecedented scale; front-page publicity for neural nets | [14][15] |
| 2012 (30 Sep) | AlexNet (Krizhevsky, Sutskever, Hinton; team "SuperVision") wins ILSVRC 2012 with 15.3% top-5 error vs 26.2% for the second-best entry; trained 5–6 days on two GTX 580 GPUs; paper at NIPS 2012. Its dropout regulariser had been posted in July (Hinton et al., arXiv 1207.0580; JMLR version 2014) | The founding event of the deep learning boom: ReLU + dropout + GPUs + big data in one package | [17][18][8][19][75] |
| 2009–2012 | Deep feed-forward acoustic models introduced by Hinton's Toronto group and Li Deng's group at Microsoft Research (2009; NIPS 2009 workshop); Hinton, Deng, Yu, Dahl et al., "Deep Neural Networks for Acoustic Modeling in Speech Recognition: The Shared Views of Four Research Groups" (*IEEE Signal Processing Magazine*, Nov 2012) | Speech is the first industry to convert — error rates fall ~30% — and Toronto, Microsoft, Google and IBM jointly declare DNNs the new standard | [1][10][11] |
| 2013 (Jan) | Mikolov, Chen, Corrado & Dean, word2vec — "Efficient Estimation of Word Representations in Vector Space" (arXiv 1301.3781, 16 Jan 2013; ICLR 2013 workshop) | Cheap, high-quality word embeddings; the Language River (file 09f) turns neural | [22][23] |
| 2013 (Dec) | Mnih et al. (DeepMind), "Playing Atari with Deep Reinforcement Learning" (arXiv 1312.5602, 19 Dec 2013) — the DQN; *Nature* version "Human-level control through deep reinforcement learning" Feb 2015 | Deep RL is born: one network, raw pixels, 49 Atari games; the RL River (file 09a) resurfaces | [25][26][69] |
| 2013 (Dec) | Kingma & Welling, "Auto-Encoding Variational Bayes" (arXiv 1312.6114, 20 Dec 2013) — the VAE; Caffe released publicly (Dec 2013) | Probabilistic deep generative models; the first widely used open CNN framework | [27][72][24] |
| 2013–2014 | Industry buys the field: Google acquires Hinton's DNNresearch Inc. (Mar 2013, reported $44M; founded 2012 with Krizhevsky and Sutskever); Yann LeCun becomes founding director of Facebook AI Research (9 Dec 2013); Google confirms acquisition of DeepMind (26 Jan 2014, reported $400–650M; founded London, Nov 2010, by Hassabis, Legg & Suleyman) | The talent land-grab that moves the field's centre of gravity into corporate labs; DeepMind gets Google-scale compute for DQN, AlphaGo and WaveNet | [20][14][59][21][64] |
| 2014 (Jun) | Goodfellow et al. (Montréal), "Generative Adversarial Networks" (arXiv 1406.2661, 10 Jun 2014) | Adversarial training; state of the art in image generation 2014–2018 and the seed of the generative-media district | [28][29][63] |
| 2014 (Sep) | Sutskever, Vinyals & Le, "Sequence to Sequence Learning with Neural Networks" (arXiv 1409.3215, 10 Sep); Bahdanau, Cho & Bengio, "Neural Machine Translation by Jointly Learning to Align and Translate" (arXiv 1409.0473, 1 Sep) — attention | Encoder–decoder LSTMs plus attention: the direct ancestors of the Transformer and of GNMT | [30][31][32] |
| 2014 (Sep) | VGG (Simonyan & Zisserman, Oxford; arXiv 1409.1556, 4 Sep) and GoogLeNet/Inception (Szegedy et al., Google; arXiv 1409.4842, 17 Sep); GoogLeNet wins ILSVRC 2014 | "Very deep" (16–22 layer) CNNs become the norm; VGG features become the universal backbone | [33][34][9] |
| 2014 (Dec) | Kingma & Ba, "Adam: A Method for Stochastic Optimization" (arXiv 1412.6980, 22 Dec 2014) | The default optimiser of the deep-learning era | [35][80] |
| 2015 (Feb) | Ioffe & Szegedy, "Batch Normalization" (arXiv 1502.03167, 11 Feb); He et al., "Delving Deep into Rectifiers" (arXiv 1502.01852, 6 Feb) report surpassing the estimated 5.1% human top-5 error on ImageNet | Training stabilisers that make very deep nets routine; "super-human" ImageNet claims begin | [36][37][76][9] |
| 2015 (Mar–Jun) | François Chollet's Keras appears on GitHub (repo created 28 Mar 2015); Highway Networks (Srivastava, Greff & Schmidhuber, arXiv 1505.00387, 3 May); U-Net (Ronneberger, Fischer & Brox, arXiv 1505.04597, 18 May); Faster R-CNN (Ren et al., arXiv 1506.01497) and YOLO (Redmon et al., arXiv 1506.02640, 8 Jun) | High-level APIs democratise the field; gated skip connections prefigure ResNet; U-Net later becomes the denoiser backbone of diffusion models; the Vision River (file 09g) moves from classification to real-time detection | [39][74][40][41] |
| 2015 (28 May) | LeCun, Bengio & Hinton, "Deep learning" review in *Nature* 521 | The founders' manifesto; the term's canonical definition | [38] |
| 2015 (9 Nov) | Google open-sources TensorFlow (Apache 2.0), successor to DistBelief; Radford, Metz & Chintala's DCGAN (arXiv 1511.06434, 19 Nov) | Industrial-grade open framework; GANs become practical for images | [44][73] |
| 2015 (Dec) | He, Zhang, Ren & Sun (Microsoft Research Asia), "Deep Residual Learning for Image Recognition" (arXiv 1512.03385, 10 Dec): 152-layer ResNet wins ILSVRC 2015 with 3.57% top-5 error; OpenAI founded as a non-profit (Dec 2015) by Musk, Altman, Sutskever, Brockman and others | Residual connections make arbitrarily deep nets trainable (and reappear in every Transformer); a third frontier lab is founded explicitly around AI's societal stakes | [42][43][9][45] |
| 2016 (27 Jan) | Silver et al. (DeepMind), "Mastering the game of Go with deep neural networks and tree search" (*Nature* 529); announcement that AlphaGo beat European champion Fan Hui 5–0 in October 2015 | First program to beat a professional on a full 19×19 board without handicap | [46][47] |
| 2016 (9–15 Mar) | AlphaGo defeats Lee Sedol 4–1 in Seoul (Lee wins game 4); Korea Baduk Association awards AlphaGo honorary 9-dan | The era's public "Deep Blue moment"; deep RL + MCTS on a problem long thought decades away | [48][47] |
| 2016 (18 May) | Google announces the Tensor Processing Unit at I/O 2016 (in production use since 2015); Nvidia's DGX-1 ships the same year | Custom accelerators for neural nets; compute becomes strategy | [49] |
| 2016 (Sep) | DeepMind's WaveNet (blog 8 Sep; arXiv 1609.03499, 12 Sep) generates raw audio; Google's GNMT paper (arXiv 1609.08144, 26 Sep) — production rollout in Google Translate from Nov 2016 | Neural TTS and neural MT at production scale; the LSTM-attention stack peaks just before the Transformer replaces it | [50][51] |
| 2017 (Jan) | PyTorch public release (v0.1.6 frozen 21 Jan 2017; first GitHub tag Sep 2016), succeeding Lua Torch; Theano's maintainers announce end of major development (28 Sep 2017) | Define-by-run frameworks win researchers; the academic frameworks of the early era give way to industrial ones | [52][53] |
| 2017 (12 Jun) | Vaswani et al., "Attention Is All You Need" (arXiv 1706.03762) — hand-off to file 07 | The Transformer: the eastern border of this continent | [55] |
| 2017 (May–Oct) | AlphaGo Master beats world No. 1 Ke Jie 3–0 at the Future of Go Summit (Wuzhen, May 2017) and DeepMind retires AlphaGo; Silver et al., "Mastering the game of Go without human knowledge" (*Nature* 550, 18 Oct 2017) — AlphaGo Zero learns from self-play only and beats AlphaGo Lee 100–0 after three days | Closes the Go chapter of the Tournament Road (file 09h); tabula-rasa RL is the era's coda and the seed of AlphaZero/MuZero | [47][54] |
| 2019 (Mar) / 2024 (Oct) | Retrospective honours: 2018 ACM Turing Award to Bengio, Hinton and LeCun (announced March 2019); 2024 Nobel Prize in Physics to Hopfield and Hinton (8 Oct 2024) | Official canonisation of the connectionist pole | [56][57][20] |

## Key people
- **Geoffrey Hinton** (University of Toronto; Google 2013–2023) — deep belief nets (2006), dropout, AlexNet supervisor, speech DNNs; co-founder of DNNresearch; 2018 Turing Award, 2024 Nobel Prize in Physics. [2][19][20][57]
- **Yann LeCun** (NYU; founding director of Facebook AI Research from 9 Dec 2013) — convolutional networks (from file 04) vindicated by AlexNet; co-author of the 2015 *Nature* review; 2018 Turing Award. [38][59]
- **Yoshua Bengio** (Université de Montréal; head of MILA) — neural language models, attention (Bahdanau et al. 2014), GANs (co-author), Theano's home lab; 2018 Turing Award. [30][28][53][60]
- **Fei-Fei Li** (Princeton → Stanford) — founded ImageNet (CVPR 2009) and, with PASCAL VOC organisers, the ILSVRC (2010–2017); director of Stanford AI Lab. [7][8][58]
- **Alex Krizhevsky & Ilya Sutskever** (Toronto → Google Brain) — built AlexNet (2012); Sutskever co-authored seq2seq (2014) and co-founded OpenAI as chief scientist (2015). [17][31][61][65]
- **Andrew Ng** (Stanford; co-founder of Google Brain 2011; chief scientist at Baidu) — GPU deep learning (Raina et al. 2009), the "cat" experiment, Coursera ML course. [6][14][62]
- **Jeff Dean & Greg Corrado** (Google) — founded Google Brain (2011), DistBelief (2012), TensorFlow (2015), word2vec co-authors. [14][16][22][81]
- **Jürgen Schmidhuber, Dan Cireşan, Ueli Meier** (IDSIA, Lugano) — GPU CNNs winning contests 2011–12; LSTM lineage from file 04. [12][13][66]
- **Demis Hassabis, Shane Legg, Mustafa Suleyman** (DeepMind, founded Nov 2010; Google from Jan 2014) — DQN, AlphaGo, WaveNet; Hassabis later shares the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry. [21][64]
- **David Silver & Volodymyr Mnih** (DeepMind) — Silver led AlphaGo/AlphaGo Zero; Mnih first-authored DQN (2013/2015). [25][26][46][54][68]
- **Ian Goodfellow** (Montréal → Google Brain → OpenAI) — inventor of GANs (2014); lead author of the *Deep Learning* textbook (MIT Press, 2016). [28][63][71]
- **Tomáš Mikolov** (Google Brain) — word2vec (2013), later at Facebook. [22][23][78]
- **Kaiming He** (Microsoft Research Asia 2011–2016 → FAIR) — PReLU/"surpassing human-level" (2015), ResNet (2015), Faster R-CNN co-author. [37][42][67]
- **Diederik Kingma** (Amsterdam → OpenAI) — VAE (with Max Welling, 2013) and Adam (with Jimmy Ba, 2014). [27][35][79]
- **François Chollet, Yangqing Jia, Soumith Chintala** — authors of Keras (2015), Caffe (Berkeley, 2013) and PyTorch (Facebook, 2017) respectively: the framework-builders who lowered the barrier to entry. [24][39][52]

## Schools of thought / sub-clusters
- **Unsupervised pre-training / energy-model school (2006–2011)** — Toronto and Montréal: deep belief nets, stacked RBMs and autoencoders, contrastive divergence; the bridge from the Boltzmann-machine country of file 04. Eclipsed after 2012 by pure supervised training with ReLU + dropout, then reborn as the VAE/GAN generative shoreline; borders: energy-based models (file 04) to the west, probabilistic graphical models of the statistical-ML era (file 05) to the south, generative modelling to the east. [2][3][4][19][77]
- **GPU supervised ConvNet school (2009–2015)** — Stanford (Raina/Ng), IDSIA (Cireşan/Schmidhuber), Toronto (AlexNet), Oxford (VGG), Google (Inception), MSRA (ResNet): win ILSVRC every year 2012–2017 and drive top-5 error from 26% to under 4%; borders: LeNet/Neocognitron lineage (file 04) to the west, the Silicon River (file 09d) running through it, Vision Transformers (file 07) to the east. [6][12][13][17][33][34][42]
- **Neural NLP / sequence school (2013–2016)** — word2vec, LSTM language models, seq2seq, attention, GNMT: the LSTM-encoder-decoder high-water mark before the Transformer; borders: statistical NLP and n-gram/SMT systems (file 05) to the west, the Transformer Plateau (file 07) to the east — the Attention River rises here. [22][30][31][32][51][70]
- **Deep reinforcement learning school (2013–2017)** — DeepMind's DQN → A3C → AlphaGo → AlphaGo Zero; deep nets as function approximators for value/policy plus Monte-Carlo tree search; borders: Sutton–Barto/TD-Gammon RL of file 04 and the RL River (file 09a) upstream, RLHF (file 07) and reasoning models (file 08) downstream; the Tournament Road (file 09h) passes through Seoul 2016. [25][26][46][54][69]
- **Deep generative modelling shoreline (2013–2017)** — VAEs (Kingma & Welling), GANs (Goodfellow), DCGAN, WaveNet's autoregressive audio; borders: the pre-training school to the west, diffusion models and the generative-media district (file 08) to the east. [27][28][50][72][73]
- **Speech-recognition converts (2009–2015)** — Toronto + Microsoft + Google + IBM DNN acoustic models, then LSTM-based recognisers (Google Voice Search 2015); the first industrial sector fully captured; borders: HMM/GMM speech systems (file 05) to the west, Whisper-style Transformer ASR (file 07) to the east. [1][10][11]
- **Tooling and infrastructure guild** — Theano (MILA), Torch7/PyTorch (NYU/Facebook), Caffe (Berkeley), Keras, DistBelief/TensorFlow, CUDA/cuDNN, TPU: the roads and ports that made the continent habitable; borders: the Commons Road (file 09e) and the Silicon River (file 09d). [5][16][24][39][44][49][52][53]
- **The industrial-lab founders (2011–2015)** — Google Brain (2011), DNNresearch acquisition (2013), FAIR (2013), DeepMind acquisition (2014), Baidu's US lab, OpenAI (2015): the migration of the field's centre of gravity from universities to corporate labs; borders: the frontier-lab archipelago of files 07–08. [14][20][21][45][59][62]

## Map notes
- Suggested A1 label: **"The Deep Learning Uplands"** (alt. "GPU Highlands", "ImageNet Escarpment"). A2 countries: *Toronto–Montréal–NYU* (the "Canadian" pre-training heartland), *Stanford/ImageNet*, *IDSIA*, *Google Brain*, *DeepMind*, *FAIR*, *OpenAI (young colony, 2015)*. A3 cities: DBN 2006, AlexNet 2012, word2vec, DQN, GAN, seq2seq/attention, VGG/GoogLeNet, ResNet, TensorFlow, AlphaGo, WaveNet/GNMT. A4 streets: ReLU, dropout, batch norm, Adam, Xavier/He initialisation, cuDNN.
- Rivers/borders/bridges: the western border with file 05 (statistical ML) should be drawn along the **ILSVRC 2012 fault line** — SVM-plus-hand-crafted-features on one side, learned features on the other — with the 2006 DBN papers as the foothills that precede it. The **Silicon River** (CUDA 2007 → GTX 580 → TPU 2016) is the continent's main waterway; the **Attention River** rises at Bahdanau 2014 and leaves the map eastward at Vaswani 2017; the **RL River** re-emerges from underground at DQN 2013 and forms the AlphaGo delta (2016–17). The **ImageNet Escarpment** (2009–2012) is the sharpest relief feature on the whole map.
- Eastern border with file 07: draw at **12 June 2017 (Transformer preprint)**, but note that AlphaGo Zero (Oct 2017) and PyTorch adoption belong culturally to this continent and should be placed as a peninsula that overlaps the border.
- Y-axis: everything here sits at the connectionist pole. The only features that reach toward the symbolic/middle bank are AlphaGo's Monte-Carlo tree search (a hybrid of learned evaluation and explicit search) and Neural Turing Machines (Graves et al., Oct 2014) — draw them as a causeway rather than a region. [46][82]
- Open questions for curation: (1) whether 2006–2011 (pre-training, GPU pioneers, speech) deserves its own A2 "foothills" region or is folded into a single 2006–2017 continent; (2) how prominently to credit IDSIA's 2011 superhuman CNN against AlexNet's 2012 fame (Schmidhuber's priority claims are a live dispute); (3) whether the 2013–2015 corporate acquisitions should be drawn as the first toll-gates on the Commons Road (file 09e); (4) whether the retrospective honours (Turing 2018, Nobel 2024) should be marked as monuments here or in the era in which they were awarded.
