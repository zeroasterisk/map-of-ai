# The Deep Learning Revolution (2006–2017)

> **Status:** reviewed
> **Date range:** 2006–2017
> **Map placement (proposed):** X = 2006–2017; Y = connectionist pole (the whole continent sits on the connectionist/statistical bank; a narrow "generative modelling" shoreline and a "deep RL" delta reach toward the middle of the map)
> **Altitude:** A1 continent ("The Deep Learning Uplands" / "GPU Highlands"), A2 country (Toronto–Montréal–NYU "Canadian Mafia", Google Brain, DeepMind, Stanford/ImageNet, IDSIA, FAIR, OpenAI), A3 city (AlexNet, ImageNet, word2vec, DQN, GAN, seq2seq/attention, ResNet, TensorFlow, AlphaGo, R-CNN/COCO, Deep Speech, OpenAI Gym), A4 street (individual tricks: ReLU, dropout, batch norm, Adam, Xavier/He init, distillation; frameworks: Theano, Caffe, Keras, Chainer, PyTorch, cuDNN)

## One-paragraph summary
Between 2006 and 2017 multi-layer neural networks went from an unfashionable niche to the default method for perception, language and control, and the phrase "deep learning" replaced "neural networks" as the banner of the connectionist pole [1]. The era opens quietly with Hinton, Osindero and Teh's greedy layer-wise pre-training of deep belief nets (2006) and the Hinton–Salakhutdinov autoencoder paper in *Science* [2][3][4]; it acquires its engine when Raina, Madhavan and Ng (2009) and Cireşan, Meier and Schmidhuber at IDSIA (2011–12) show that Nvidia GPUs and CUDA make training large convolutional nets practical, and its fuel when Fei-Fei Li's group releases ImageNet (CVPR 2009) and launches the ILSVRC challenge (2010) [5][6][7][8][12][13]. The decisive event is AlexNet at ILSVRC 2012 — a top-5 error of 15.3% against 26.2% for the runner-up — which, together with the same year's Google Brain "cat" experiment and the deep-nets-for-speech consensus paper, convinced industry to buy the field: Google acquired Hinton's DNNresearch (March 2013) and DeepMind (January 2014), Facebook hired LeCun to found FAIR (December 2013), Baidu hired Ng, and OpenAI was founded (December 2015) [11][15][17][18][20][21][45][59][62]. In the five years that followed, nearly the whole vocabulary of modern deep learning was written: word2vec (2013), DQN (2013/2015), VAEs and GANs (2013–14), seq2seq and Bahdanau attention (2014), VGG/GoogLeNet/ResNet (2014–15), Adam and batch normalisation (2014–15), open frameworks (Theano, Caffe, Keras, TensorFlow, PyTorch), and custom silicon (Google's TPU) [22][25][27][28][30][31][33][34][35][36][42][44][49][52]. AlphaGo's 4–1 defeat of Lee Sedol in March 2016 was the era's Deep-Blue moment and AlphaGo Zero (October 2017) its coda; two months earlier, in June 2017, eight Google researchers had posted "Attention Is All You Need", the Transformer paper that hands the map over to the next continent (file 07) [46][48][54][55]. The founders' achievement was canonised retroactively — the 2018 Turing Award to Bengio, Hinton and LeCun, and the 2024 Nobel Prize in Physics to Hopfield and Hinton [56][57].

## Schools of thought / sub-clusters
- **Unsupervised pre-training / energy-model school (2006–2011)** — Toronto and Montréal: deep belief nets, stacked RBMs and autoencoders, contrastive divergence; the bridge from the Boltzmann-machine country of file 04. Eclipsed after 2012 by pure supervised training with ReLU + Xavier/He initialisation + dropout (Glorot & Bengio 2010, Nair & Hinton 2010), then reborn as the VAE/GAN generative shoreline; the CIFAR NCAP programme (2004–) is the institution that held Toronto, Montréal and NYU together through the lean years; borders: energy-based models (file 04) to the west, probabilistic graphical models of the statistical-ML era (file 05) to the south, generative modelling to the east. [2][3][4][83][19][77][84][85][107]
- **GPU supervised ConvNet school (2009–2015)** — Stanford (Raina/Ng), IDSIA (Cireşan/Schmidhuber), Toronto (AlexNet), Oxford (VGG), Google (Inception), MSRA (ResNet): win ILSVRC every year 2012–2017 and drive top-5 error from 26% to 2.25% (SENet, the final 2017 edition); its detection/segmentation province — Berkeley's R-CNN family and FCN, COCO (2014), YOLO, U-Net — turns classification backbones into general vision, and its by-products (DeepFace, DeepDream, style transfer, adversarial examples) are the era's first public and first cautionary artefacts; borders: LeNet/Neocognitron lineage (file 04) to the west, the Silicon River (file 09d) running through it, Vision Transformers (file 07) to the east. [6][12][13][17][33][34][42][9][89][90][91][114][96]
- **Neural NLP / sequence school (2011–2016)** — Collobert & Weston's "NLP from scratch" (2011), word2vec and GloVe, LSTM language models, GRU encoder–decoders, seq2seq, attention, image captioning, Memory Networks / Neural Turing Machines / DNC (2014–16), GNMT: the LSTM-encoder-decoder high-water mark before the Transformer; borders: statistical NLP and n-gram/SMT systems (file 05) to the west, the Transformer Plateau (file 07) to the east — the Attention River rises here. [87][22][88][104][30][31][32][98][118][82][51][70]
- **Deep reinforcement learning school (2013–2017)** — DeepMind's DQN → A3C (2016) → AlphaGo → AlphaGo Zero, with OpenAI Gym (2016) as the shared arena; deep nets as function approximators for value/policy plus Monte-Carlo tree search; borders: Sutton–Barto/TD-Gammon RL of file 04 and the RL River (file 09a) upstream, RLHF (file 07) and reasoning models (file 08) downstream; the Tournament Road (file 09h) passes through Seoul 2016. [25][26][99][100][46][54][69]
- **Deep generative modelling shoreline (2013–2017)** — VAEs (Kingma & Welling), GANs (Goodfellow), DCGAN, the PixelRNN/PixelCNN/WaveNet autoregressive family, and neural style transfer as the public-facing outlier; borders: the pre-training school to the west, diffusion models and the generative-media district (file 08) to the east. [27][28][103][50][72][73][101]
- **Speech-recognition converts (2009–2016)** — Toronto + Microsoft + Google + IBM DNN acoustic models, then LSTM/CTC recognisers (Graves 2013; Google Voice Search 2015), Baidu's end-to-end Deep Speech (2014) and Microsoft's Switchboard human-parity claim (Oct 2016); the first industrial sector fully captured; borders: HMM/GMM speech systems (file 05) to the west, Whisper-style Transformer ASR (file 07) to the east. [1][10][11][105][93][95]
- **Tooling and infrastructure guild** — Theano (MILA), Torch7/PyTorch (NYU/Facebook), Caffe (Berkeley), Keras, Chainer (Tokyo), DistBelief/TensorFlow, CUDA/cuDNN (2014), DGX-1 (2016), TPU (2016), Volta Tensor Cores (2017), plus the Kaggle competition circuit and ICLR (2013) as the guild's market and meeting hall; borders: the Commons Road (file 09e) and the Silicon River (file 09d). [5][16][24][39][113][44][92][115][116][49][52][53][86][106]
- **The industrial-lab founders (2011–2015)** — Google Brain (2011), DNNresearch acquisition (2013), FAIR (2013), DeepMind acquisition (2014), Baidu Research under Ng (May 2014), OpenAI (2015): the migration of the field's centre of gravity from universities to corporate labs; borders: the frontier-lab archipelago of files 07–08. [14][20][21][45][59][62][94]
- **The Chinese and state response (2014–2017)** — Baidu Research/Deep Speech (2014), the computer-vision start-ups SenseTime (Hong Kong/CUHK, Oct 2014; ILSVRC 2016 detection winner) and Megvii/Face++ (Beijing, Face++ launched 2012), and, at the era's close, the first national AI strategies — the US *Preparing for the Future of AI* report (Oct 2016), Canada's Pan-Canadian strategy and Vector Institute (2017), China's New Generation AI Development Plan (Jul 2017); borders: this continent's eastern coast, the policy coast of files 07–08 and the Conscience Road (file 09c). [93][94][110][111][117][107][109][112]

## Map notes
- Suggested A1 label: **"The Deep Learning Uplands"** (alt. "GPU Highlands", "ImageNet Escarpment"). A2 countries: *Toronto–Montréal–NYU* (the "Canadian" pre-training heartland), *Stanford/ImageNet*, *IDSIA*, *Google Brain*, *DeepMind*, *FAIR*, *OpenAI (young colony, 2015)*. A3 cities: DBN 2006, AlexNet 2012, word2vec, DQN, GAN, seq2seq/attention, VGG/GoogLeNet, ResNet, TensorFlow, AlphaGo, WaveNet/GNMT; added by review: Kaggle-Merck 2012 (the "first commercial conversion" waypoint), R-CNN/COCO 2013–14 (detection province capital), Deep Speech 2014 (Baidu's first city), Show and Tell 2014 (vision–language bridgehead), adversarial examples 2013–14 (a marked hazard, not a city), style transfer/DeepDream 2015 (public shoreline), A3C/OpenAI Gym 2016 (deep-RL delta), Switchboard parity 2016. A4 streets: ReLU, dropout, batch norm/layer norm, Adam, Xavier/He initialisation, distillation, GRU, cuDNN.
- Rivers/borders/bridges: the western border with file 05 (statistical ML) should be drawn along the **ILSVRC 2012 fault line** — SVM-plus-hand-crafted-features on one side, learned features on the other — with the 2006 DBN papers as the foothills that precede it. The **Silicon River** (CUDA 2007 → GTX 580 → TPU 2016) is the continent's main waterway; the **Attention River** rises at Bahdanau 2014 and leaves the map eastward at Vaswani 2017; the **RL River** re-emerges from underground at DQN 2013 and forms the AlphaGo delta (2016–17). The **ImageNet Escarpment** (2009–2012) is the sharpest relief feature on the whole map.
- Eastern border with file 07: draw at **12 June 2017 (Transformer preprint)**, but note that AlphaGo Zero (Oct 2017) and PyTorch adoption belong culturally to this continent and should be placed as a peninsula that overlaps the border. The **national-strategy forts** (US report Oct 2016, Pan-Canadian/Vector 2017, China's plan Jul 2017) stand on that same coast: they are reactions to this continent but their consequences (funding, chip export politics) play out in files 07–08.
- Non-US/Canada/UK geography that Phase 2 should not flatten: Tübingen (style transfer), Freiburg (U-Net), Oxford (VGG), Amsterdam (VAE), Lugano (IDSIA), Tokyo (Chainer/Preferred Networks), Beijing/Sunnyvale (Baidu Research) and Hong Kong/Beijing (SenseTime, Megvii) — the continent is Anglo-Canadian in its capitals but not in its towns.
- Y-axis: everything here sits at the connectionist pole. The only features that reach toward the symbolic/middle bank are AlphaGo's Monte-Carlo tree search (a hybrid of learned evaluation and explicit search) and Neural Turing Machines (Graves et al., Oct 2014) — draw them as a causeway rather than a region. [46][82]
- Open questions for curation: (1) whether 2006–2011 (pre-training, GPU pioneers, speech) deserves its own A2 "foothills" region or is folded into a single 2006–2017 continent; (2) how prominently to credit IDSIA's 2011 superhuman CNN against AlexNet's 2012 fame (Schmidhuber's priority claims are a live dispute); (3) whether the 2013–2015 corporate acquisitions should be drawn as the first toll-gates on the Commons Road (file 09e); (4) whether the retrospective honours (Turing 2018, Nobel 2024) should be marked as monuments here or in the era in which they were awarded.
