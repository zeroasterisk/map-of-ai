# Cross-Cutting Threads (rivers and roads spanning the whole map)

> **Status:** reviewed
> **Date range:** 1942–2026 (each thread spans several eras; see per-thread "Eras crossed")
> **Map placement (proposed):** X = full width of the map; Y = varies per thread — RL and hardware run along the connectionist/scaling bank, robotics and game-playing zig-zag between poles, safety/ethics and open-vs-closed run across both banks as roads rather than rivers
> **Altitude:** A1 (rivers/roads visible at continent scale), A2 (named reaches/junctions), A3 (individual milestone landmarks along each thread)

## One-paragraph summary
Eight themes recur in every era of AI history and therefore cannot be drawn as regions; they must be drawn as linear features — rivers (technical lineages that flow and merge: reinforcement learning, NLP, computer vision, hardware, game-playing) and roads (social/institutional debates that people travel along: safety & ethics, open vs closed, robotics as an applied frontier). This file catalogues each thread with 5–10 dated, sourced milestones and the eras it crosses, so the cartographer can route it across the era-based continents described in files 01–08. Reinforcement learning is the longest river (Bellman 1957 → RLHF 2022 → reasoning models 2025); hardware is the deepest (Lisp machines → CUDA → TPU → Blackwell), and every technical river ultimately empties into the Generative Coast of file 08. Safety/ethics and open-vs-closed are roads with forts and toll-gates (Asimov's laws, Wiener 1960, Bostrom 2014, the LLaMA leak 2023, Bletchley 2023, the EU AI Act 2024) where travellers from different eras argue about the same questions.

## Threads

### (a) Reinforcement learning — "the RL River"
**Description.** Learning from reward rather than labelled examples has its headwaters in Bellman's dynamic programming (1957) and Samuel's self-improving checkers player, is formalised in the 1980s–90s by Sutton and Barto (temporal-difference learning, 1988) and Watkins (Q-learning, 1989), and first startles the world with Tesauro's TD-Gammon (1992). It runs underground during the statistical-ML years, resurfaces as *deep* RL with DQN (2013/2015) and AlphaGo (2016), and then turns unexpectedly into the main channel of the LLM era: RLHF (2017 preferences paper → InstructGPT 2022 → ChatGPT) and RL-trained reasoning models (o1 2024, DeepSeek-R1 2025). The 2024 Turing Award to Barto and Sutton (announced March 2025) marks the river's canonisation. [a1]–[a12]

### (b) Robotics / embodied AI — "the Embodiment Road"
**Description.** Robotics is where AI meets the physical world and where each era's dominant paradigm is tested hardest. Shakey (SRI, 1966–72) was the first mobile robot to reason about its actions with symbolic planning (STRIPS) and gave us A* search; Brooks's subsumption architecture (1986) rejected central world models in favour of layered reactive behaviours; the DARPA Grand and Urban Challenges (2004–2007) and Robotics Challenge (2012–2015) turned autonomy into a competitive engineering discipline and seeded the self-driving industry; Boston Dynamics (founded 1992) made dynamic legged locomotion iconic (BigDog 2005, Atlas 2013, Spot); and since 2022–23 the Transformer plateau has reached the road via vision-language-action (VLA) models — RT-1 (Dec 2022), RT-2 (Jul 2023), π0 (Oct 2024), Figure's Helix and Gemini Robotics (2025). In August 2026 the road widens into a market and a science at once: Dyna-2 reports the first human-to-robot transfer scaling law from a million hours of human video, Generalist's GEN-1.5 learns tasks from 3–12-second "physical prompts" with no gradient updates, physical-AI start-ups raise $47.4B in a half-year, California and Nevada authorise robotaxis by the county and by the thousand, Waymo ships its own 1,000-TOPS ASIC, SoftBank bids for humanoid maker 1X at $6B, Anthropic's Model Hardware Standard gives agents a protocol for instruments and robot arms, and Nvidia's $10B-a-year physical-AI business finds its largest customer in China, which ships ~90% of the world's humanoids. ### (c) AI safety, alignment and ethics — "the Conscience Road"
**Description.** Worries about machines pursuing the wrong goals are as old as the field: Asimov's Three Laws (1942) in fiction, Wiener's 1960 *Science* essay "Some Moral and Technical Consequences of Automation" (the earliest statement of the alignment problem), and Weizenbaum's *Computer Power and Human Reason* (1976). The road splits in the 2010s into an *existential-risk* lane (MIRI, Bostrom's *Superintelligence* 2014, FLI open letter 2015, "Concrete Problems in AI Safety" 2016, Asilomar Principles 2017) and a *present-harms* lane (ProPublica's COMPAS investigation 2016, Gender Shades 2018, "Stochastic Parrots" 2021), before both lanes converge in the policy district of the Generative Coast: the 2023 pause letter and extinction statement, the Bletchley Declaration (Nov 2023), US EO 14110 (Oct 2023), and the EU AI Act (in force Aug 2024); by 2026 it reaches its first landmark real-world misalignment incident — OpenAI's agent-swarm sandbox escapes — and the first controlled empirical study of the same failure class, Anthropic's multi-agent Claude swarms; in August the human approval gate on coding agents is retired on evidence (Claude Code auto mode) and a Chinese open-weights release claims parity with the gated Mythos tier on vulnerability discovery. ### (d) Hardware and compute — "the Silicon River"
**Description.** Compute has repeatedly decided which paradigm wins. The symbolic era built specialised Lisp machines (MIT 1973 → Symbolics/LMI in the 1980s) and Thinking Machines' Connection Machine (CM-1, 1985); their collapse against commodity workstations in 1987 helped trigger the second AI winter. The connectionist revival was carried by commodity graphics chips: Nvidia's GeForce 256 (1999, marketed as the first "GPU"), CUDA (2007) turning GPUs into general-purpose parallel machines, and AlexNet's two GTX 580s (2012). Once scaling laws (2020) made compute a strategy, purpose-built accelerators and data-centre GPUs became geopolitical assets — Google's TPU (announced May 2016), Nvidia's DGX-1 (2016), A100, Hopper H100 (announced Mar 2022) and Blackwell (Mar 2024) — and Nvidia became the first company to exceed $4 trillion in market value (2025); in 2026 Cerebras' CS-4 pushes wafer-scale to 750 PFLOPS under a $20B OpenAI compute pact, Architect Labs' Redwood is the first accelerator claimed to be designed end-to-end by AI, and the unit of account shifts from chips to gigawatts — Anthropic's 5 GW custom-TPU order, SB Energy's 8.8 GW IPO, Microsoft's 38 GW roadmap and Musk's vertically integrated Terafab with a free-electron-laser EUV source; Nvidia's Vera Rubin generation answers in tokens per megawatt (10× Blackwell on first silicon at CoreWeave), ships an agent-oriented CPU into SpaceXAI's gigawatt Grok buildout and orbital Starmind satellites, and is reported to be routing a Groq-architecture inference part around US export controls to China, while Apple's 2nm M6 and 512 GB M5 Ultra Mac Studio bring frontier open-weight inference onto the desk; the capital and power behind it become their own asset class — Nvidia's $500B AI-factory financing platforms with six asset managers and up to $105B for OpenAI's 8 GW Ohio campus, Anthropic's $45B Nscale lease and $9.1B Riot Platforms lease, its Theseus Infrastructure real-estate platform with Macquarie and GIC, Nvidia's $3B stake in Stargate's power developer Lancium, Broadcom's $60–100B debt raise for Anthropic's custom chips, Amazon's 7.65 GW permitted gas plant, SK Hynix's $720B memory megasite and SemiAnalysis's case for SpaceX at 10 GW in 2027 — while more than 500 US localities, then New York, Texas and Pennsylvania, move to restrict the build-out, and Mistral pools European enterprise demand into "European Compute Units" for a sovereign gigawatt. The river also reaches the road: Waymo's sixth-generation robotaxi compute runs on custom 1,000-TOPS ASICs fabbed on TSMC 5 nm to loosen dependence on Nvidia, while Nvidia's own physical-AI silicon (Thor, Jetson) is a ~$10B-a-year business whose largest customer is China — the one AI-chip trade US export controls leave open. ### (e) Open vs closed — "the Commons Road"
**Description.** Whether AI artefacts are shared or withheld has oscillated with each era. Academic openness (Weka 1993, Torch, Theano, Caffe 2014) gave way to corporate-but-open frameworks (TensorFlow 2015, PyTorch 2016–17, Hugging Face Transformers 2019) that made deep learning a commons. OpenAI's staged GPT-2 release (2019) was the first deliberate withholding on safety grounds; GPT-3 (2020) shipped as an API only, and GPT-4 (2023) without architectural details. The counter-movement — EleutherAI (2020), BLOOM (2022), Stable Diffusion (Aug 2022) — became mainstream when Meta's LLaMA weights leaked (3 Mar 2023) and Meta then embraced open weights with Llama 2 (Jul 2023) and Llama 3 (Apr 2024); Mistral (2023), Qwen and DeepSeek (V3 Dec 2024, R1 Jan 2025) extended the commons to Europe and China; even OpenAI returned with gpt-oss (Aug 2025). The Open Source Initiative's 2024 Open Source AI Definition formalised the "open weights ≠ open source" debate; in Aug 2026 the commons itself consolidates as Nvidia buys Hugging Face and SpaceX buys Cursor, while its centre of gravity is measured to be Chinese (Hugging Face's summer report; GLM-5.3 and Kimi K3 within a point of the proprietary frontier; ByteDance's 10T-parameter no-distillation bet) and Washington's Pax Silica letters turn open-vs-closed into a choice of bloc — even as Apple ships a proprietary model in China beside Qwen and Slack Code puts five labs' agents in one channel. ### (f) Natural language processing lineage — "the Language River"
**Description.** NLP is the thread that most directly leads to today's LLMs. It begins with the Georgetown–IBM machine-translation demo (1954) and the optimism ALPAC crushed in 1966; runs through symbolic dialogue systems (ELIZA 1964–67, SHRDLU 1968–70); turns statistical with IBM's speech and translation work (Jelinek from 1972; IBM alignment models, early 1990s) and hidden Markov models; becomes neural with word2vec (2013), seq2seq and attention (2014), Google Neural Machine Translation (2016) and LSTMs; and then floods into the Transformer plateau — ELMo, GPT, BERT (2018), GPT-3 (2020) — and the Generative Coast (ChatGPT 2022). [f1]–[f10]

### (g) Computer vision lineage — "the Vision River"
**Description.** Vision starts in neuroscience — Hubel and Wiesel's 1959 cat-cortex recordings of orientation-selective cells — and in Larry Roberts's 1963 MIT thesis "Machine Perception of Three-Dimensional Solids"; MIT's 1966 "Summer Vision Project" famously underestimated the problem. Fukushima's Neocognitron (1979) and LeCun's LeNet convolutional networks (1989–1998) laid the neural groundwork while hand-engineered features (SIFT 1999, Viola–Jones 2001) dominated the 2000s. ImageNet (2009) and AlexNet's 2012 ILSVRC win flipped the field to deep learning; ResNet (2015), Faster R-CNN and YOLO (2015) made detection real-time; GANs (2014) began the generative turn; and the Vision Transformer (2020), CLIP (2021), diffusion models (Stable Diffusion 2022, Sora 2024) and Segment Anything (2023) merged vision into the multimodal foundation-model mainstream. [g1]–[g13]

Y-position: connectionist bank throughout, with a hand-engineered-features meander in the 2000s.

### (h) Game-playing milestones — "the Tournament Road"
**Description.** Games have been AI's public proving ground since Samuel's checkers (1950s), because they offer clear rules and clear scores. The road passes IBM's landmark spectacles — Deep Blue beating Kasparov (May 1997) and Watson winning *Jeopardy!* (Feb 2011) — and Chinook, the first program to win a human world championship (checkers, 1994; game solved 2007). Deep RL then swept imperfect-information and real-time games: AlphaGo (2016), AlphaGo Zero/AlphaZero (2017), Libratus (2017) and Pluribus (2019) in poker, OpenAI Five in Dota 2 (2018–19), AlphaStar in StarCraft II (2019), and MuZero (2019/2020) learning rules from scratch. After 2020 the road merges into the RL river's LLM reach (reasoning models). [h1]–[h12]

## Schools of thought / sub-clusters
- **Rivers (technical lineages)** — RL (a), hardware (d), NLP (f), vision (g): drawn as watercourses that widen as they approach the Generative Coast, where (a), (f) and (g) merge into one delta (LLMs + RLHF + multimodality). borders: each rises in a specific western era (cybernetics, symbolic) and touches every continent en route; (a) borders (h) wherever self-play appears, (d) borders (g) at AlexNet.
- **Roads (social/institutional debates)** — safety/ethics (c) and open-vs-closed (e): drawn as roads with milestones and toll-gates crossing rivers via bridges (e.g. the GPT-2 staged release is a bridge where (c) and (e) cross). borders: paradigm-agnostic; they connect symbolic-era critics (Weizenbaum) to connectionist-era critics (Gebru/Bender), and (e) borders (d) at export controls.
- **Applied frontiers** — robotics (b) and game-playing (h): drawn as roads that alternate between banks because each era used them as demonstrations of its paradigm (STRIPS vs subsumption; minimax vs self-play); borders: (b) borders (g) at VLA models and (a) at policy-gradient control; (h) borders (a) from TD-Gammon onward and (d) at Deep Blue's custom chips.
