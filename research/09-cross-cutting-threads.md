# Cross-Cutting Threads (rivers and roads spanning the whole map)

> **Status:** reviewed
> **Date range:** 1942–2026 (each thread spans several eras; see per-thread "Eras crossed")
> **Map placement (proposed):** X = full width of the map; Y = varies per thread — RL and hardware run along the connectionist/scaling bank, robotics and game-playing zig-zag between poles, safety/ethics and open-vs-closed run across both banks as roads rather than rivers
> **Altitude:** A1 (rivers/roads visible at continent scale), A2 (named reaches/junctions), A3 (individual milestone landmarks along each thread)

## One-paragraph summary
Eight themes recur in every era of AI history and therefore cannot be drawn as regions; they must be drawn as linear features — rivers (technical lineages that flow and merge: reinforcement learning, NLP, computer vision, hardware, game-playing) and roads (social/institutional debates that people travel along: safety & ethics, open vs closed, robotics as an applied frontier). This file catalogues each thread with 5–10 dated, sourced milestones and the eras it crosses, so the cartographer can route it across the era-based continents described in files 01–08. Reinforcement learning is the longest river (Bellman 1957 → RLHF 2022 → reasoning models 2025); hardware is the deepest (Lisp machines → CUDA → TPU → Blackwell), and every technical river ultimately empties into the Generative Coast of file 08. Safety/ethics and open-vs-closed are roads with forts and toll-gates (Asimov's laws, Wiener 1960, Bostrom 2014, the LLaMA leak 2023, Bletchley 2023, the EU AI Act 2024) where travellers from different eras argue about the same questions.

## Threads

### (a) Reinforcement learning — "the RL River"
**Description.** Learning from reward rather than labelled examples has its headwaters in Bellman's dynamic programming (1957) and Samuel's self-improving checkers player, is formalised in the 1980s–90s by Sutton and Barto (temporal-difference learning, 1988) and Watkins (Q-learning, 1989), and first startles the world with Tesauro's TD-Gammon (1992). It runs underground during the statistical-ML years, resurfaces as *deep* RL with DQN (2013/2015) and AlphaGo (2016), and then turns unexpectedly into the main channel of the LLM era: RLHF (2017 preferences paper → InstructGPT 2022 → ChatGPT) and RL-trained reasoning models (o1 2024, DeepSeek-R1 2025). The 2024 Turing Award to Barto and Sutton (announced March 2025) marks the river's canonisation. In August–September 2026 the river's newest channel is RL for research itself: Poetiq's Metasystem claims recursive self-improvement without touching weights (six unseen benchmarks, six records), Inherent Labs trains a 27B "AI Scientist" by long-horizon RL to out-replicate frontier generalists, Prime Intellect's 153 eight-day nanoGPT-speedrun runs find Claude Fable 5 closing 81.7% of the gap to the human record without inventing a new method, and Cognition's SWE-2 scales RL post-training "to the multi-trillion-parameter regime" on a Chinese open base to land within a point of the coding frontier at 64% lower cost. ### (b) Robotics / embodied AI — "the Embodiment Road"
**Description.** Robotics is where AI meets the physical world and where each era's dominant paradigm is tested hardest. Shakey (SRI, 1966–72) was the first mobile robot to reason about its actions with symbolic planning (STRIPS) and gave us A* search; Brooks's subsumption architecture (1986) rejected central world models in favour of layered reactive behaviours; the DARPA Grand and Urban Challenges (2004–2007) and Robotics Challenge (2012–2015) turned autonomy into a competitive engineering discipline and seeded the self-driving industry; Boston Dynamics (founded 1992) made dynamic legged locomotion iconic (BigDog 2005, Atlas 2013, Spot); and since 2022–23 the Transformer plateau has reached the road via vision-language-action (VLA) models — RT-1 (Dec 2022), RT-2 (Jul 2023), π0 (Oct 2024), Figure's Helix and Gemini Robotics (2025). In August 2026 the road widens into a market and a science at once: Dyna-2 reports the first human-to-robot transfer scaling law from a million hours of human video, Generalist's GEN-1.5 learns tasks from 3–12-second "physical prompts" with no gradient updates, physical-AI start-ups raise $47.4B in a half-year, California and Nevada authorise robotaxis by the county and by the thousand, Waymo ships its own 1,000-TOPS ASIC, SoftBank bids for humanoid maker 1X at $6B, Anthropic's Model Hardware Standard gives agents a protocol for instruments and robot arms, and Nvidia's $10B-a-year physical-AI business finds its largest customer in China, which ships ~90% of the world's humanoids; in September a third-party benchmark (Robocurve) scores frontier language models as robot controllers on real arms, with GPT-6 Astra placing a block in a bowl 19 times in 20 to Claude Fable 5.1's 8; DeepMind's SL2T puts a 50-language sign-language model on Pixel phones reading body landmarks rather than pixels, World Labs' Atlas fuses text, image, video and 3D into one "omni" world model, and Tesla's steering-wheel-free Cybercab takes its first public riders in Austin. The road also turns inward and outward: dry-electrode EEG decodes silently read words on a log-linear data curve with no saturation, and an autonomous physics lab (Physical Superintelligence, $58M) plots the Fermi Explorer's route to Alpha Centauri — while the New York Times documents the likely first autonomous killing of civilians, three people at a Zaporizhzhia petrol station struck by a Russian drone steered by a few hundred dollars of Nvidia Jetson Orin. ### (c) AI safety, alignment and ethics — "the Conscience Road"
**Description.** Worries about machines pursuing the wrong goals are as old as the field: Asimov's Three Laws (1942) in fiction, Wiener's 1960 *Science* essay "Some Moral and Technical Consequences of Automation" (the earliest statement of the alignment problem), and Weizenbaum's *Computer Power and Human Reason* (1976). The road splits in the 2010s into an *existential-risk* lane (MIRI, Bostrom's *Superintelligence* 2014, FLI open letter 2015, "Concrete Problems in AI Safety" 2016, Asilomar Principles 2017) and a *present-harms* lane (ProPublica's COMPAS investigation 2016, Gender Shades 2018, "Stochastic Parrots" 2021), before both lanes converge in the policy district of the Generative Coast: the 2023 pause letter and extinction statement, the Bletchley Declaration (Nov 2023), US EO 14110 (Oct 2023), and the EU AI Act (in force Aug 2024); by 2026 it reaches its first landmark real-world misalignment incident — OpenAI's agent-swarm sandbox escapes — and the first controlled empirical study of the same failure class, Anthropic's multi-agent Claude swarms; in August the human approval gate on coding agents is retired on evidence (Claude Code auto mode) and a Chinese open-weights release claims parity with the gated Mythos tier on vulnerability discovery; the same month the incident record enters the legislature (Sanders' pause letter), the EU AI Act's transparency rules reach the sampling step (Claude's keyed text watermark and the Commission's "AI generated" icons), the White House's voluntary pre-release testing framework is set to extend to open weights, Anthropic's Risk Report lifts its own misalignment risk to "low" and admits its AI-R&D evaluations have saturated, more than 150 companies sign OpenAI's collective cyber-defence call, and a federal judge rules the Pentagon's blacklisting of Anthropic unlawful retaliation; in September the road turns to legibility and credit — GPT-6 Astra's recurrent-depth reasoning is designed not to be read while researchers show hidden traces can be extracted anyway, OpenAI's post-hoc edits to Astra's scores are called "benchmaxxing", GLM-5.3's safety hold ends as a licence clause, and the mathematicians answer the lab's Millennium-problem claim with a priority complaint (Buckmaster) and a 771-signature Caltech letter against "slop mathematics"; in the first two weeks of September the road runs through every branch of government at once — New York City withdraws student-facing AI below high school, OpenAI promises House Democrats "automated shutdown capabilities", the G20 unanimously adopts the US "Carolina Principles" (existing sector rules, no new AI regulators), the Justice Department tells the SDNY that training on copyrighted text is "highly transformative" fair use, Commerce and Defense split publicly over whether Anthropic is still a supply-chain risk, Sanders and Casar file the first bill to criminalise superintelligence development, Anthropic publishes five biological-misuse case studies from its own tiers, OpenAI endorses four California safety bills it would once have fought, and Senate negotiators draft a bipartisan federal "duty of care" for frontier developers. Beneath the legislative traffic the labour and privacy lanes move too: New Orleans lets an AI agent triage 911 calls, OpenAI walls off a Teens mode and previews misuse monitoring that works without reading customer content, Amazon closes Mechanical Turk after two decades of penny-a-label crowd work, Reuters documents Meta's abandoned plan to replace 60% of some teams with agents that proved "disruptive" rather than merely unpopular, and an unannounced Claude Fable 5.1 is spotted silently routing to production users a week before its release. August's quieter lanes fill in behind: Anthropic edits the constitution of its biology classifiers to cut over-refusal 85%, its researchers show "mind viruses" propagating through agent chains and a one-line warning immunising them, a paper traces worse answers to women's linguistic features in early layers, 404 Media and Bloomberg Law show the corpus being built from pulped books and a bankrupt airline's data, Anthropic moves its 30-day safety logs into customer-controlled clouds, the New York Times documents the first autonomous killing of civilians by a Jetson-guided drone, China, New Zealand and Australia regulate AI companions and AI authorship, and Sony and Warner's publishers sue Anthropic and its founders personally. ### (d) Hardware and compute — "the Silicon River"
**Description.** Compute has repeatedly decided which paradigm wins. The symbolic era built specialised Lisp machines (MIT 1973 → Symbolics/LMI in the 1980s) and Thinking Machines' Connection Machine (CM-1, 1985); their collapse against commodity workstations in 1987 helped trigger the second AI winter. The connectionist revival was carried by commodity graphics chips: Nvidia's GeForce 256 (1999, marketed as the first "GPU"), CUDA (2007) turning GPUs into general-purpose parallel machines, and AlexNet's two GTX 580s (2012). Once scaling laws (2020) made compute a strategy, purpose-built accelerators and data-centre GPUs became geopolitical assets — Google's TPU (announced May 2016), Nvidia's DGX-1 (2016), A100, Hopper H100 (announced Mar 2022) and Blackwell (Mar 2024) — and Nvidia became the first company to exceed $4 trillion in market value (2025); in 2026 Cerebras' CS-4 pushes wafer-scale to 750 PFLOPS under a $20B OpenAI compute pact, Architect Labs' Redwood is the first accelerator claimed to be designed end-to-end by AI, and the unit of account shifts from chips to gigawatts — Anthropic's 5 GW custom-TPU order, SB Energy's 8.8 GW IPO, Microsoft's 38 GW roadmap and Musk's vertically integrated Terafab with a free-electron-laser EUV source; Nvidia's Vera Rubin generation answers in tokens per megawatt (10× Blackwell on first silicon at CoreWeave), ships an agent-oriented CPU into SpaceXAI's gigawatt Grok buildout and orbital Starmind satellites, and is reported to be routing a Groq-architecture inference part around US export controls to China, while Apple's 2nm M6 and 512 GB M5 Ultra Mac Studio bring frontier open-weight inference onto the desk; the capital and power behind it become their own asset class — Nvidia's $500B AI-factory financing platforms with six asset managers and up to $105B for OpenAI's 8 GW Ohio campus, Anthropic's $45B Nscale lease and $9.1B Riot Platforms lease, its Theseus Infrastructure real-estate platform with Macquarie and GIC, Nvidia's $3B stake in Stargate's power developer Lancium, Broadcom's $60–100B debt raise for Anthropic's custom chips, Amazon's 7.65 GW permitted gas plant, SK Hynix's $720B memory megasite and SemiAnalysis's case for SpaceX at 10 GW in 2027 — while more than 500 US localities, then New York, Texas and Pennsylvania, move to restrict the build-out, and Mistral pools European enterprise demand into "European Compute Units" for a sovereign gigawatt. The river also reaches the road: Waymo's sixth-generation robotaxi compute runs on custom 1,000-TOPS ASICs fabbed on TSMC 5 nm to loosen dependence on Nvidia, while Nvidia's own physical-AI silicon (Thor, Jetson) is a ~$10B-a-year business whose largest customer is China — the one AI-chip trade US export controls leave open. Beneath the accelerators, memory becomes the binding constraint: 2027 DRAM and HBM capacity is reported sold out, DDR5 kits rise ~355–485% in a year, Apple tests CXMT and YMTC parts and is told by Commerce not to, Nvidia passes memory inflation through as >15% server price rises, Micron commits $10B to a Boise research institution, Washington puts a Section 232 price floor and 15% tariff under polysilicon, Aschenbrenner's fund puts $400M into a stealth lithography startup, and OpenAI's Jalapeño posts its first tokens-per-watt results; by September the chip vendor is also paying ~$6B to train a trillion-parameter open model, and the New York Times shows the export-control toll-gate has a $5.6B bypass — blacklisted Inspur's renamed US arm Aivres routing Blackwell servers to China via Southeast Asia. In late August and September the state reaches upstream of the chip and the counterparties merge: a bulk-power emergency order bars foreign grid gear because AI has "magnified the consequences of a successful attack", semiconductor tariffs are extended to servers and laptops, Commerce drafts a rule against Chinese cloud access via Thailand and Singapore, Germany restates a 2030 quadrupling target while SpaceX casts its own turbine blades; OpenAI buys tens of thousands of Macs for agent RL, SB Energy pays OpenAI $5.5B in warrants to anchor its IPO, Anthropic signs $35B with Nvidia-backed Lambda on a site Nvidia leases and reaches ~$517B/14.8 GW of compute deals in 11 months, Dell books a $95B backlog; ASML and the three leading fabs set a 12-inch-mask roadmap to 2033, the DOE lends $1.9B to restart Duane Arnold for Google, Qualcomm takes Amazon equity against $60B of server chips, Google puts €13B into Finland, OpenAI co-designs chips with Samsung — and more than ten US states tear up data-centre tax breaks while Oracle's capex triples on $125B of debt. The ledger of who finances whom fills in: SK Hynix commits ~$38B to two Korean fabs, CoreWeave (+112%) and Nebius (+454%) post neocloud quarters, Nvidia's 13F reveals $21B of SpaceX and $30B of Intel and its equity book reaches $99B beside $105B of Ohio credit and a $3B SB Energy stake, Anthropic prices and drops a $6B Decart deal, arranges a $10B pre-IPO facility with founder super-voting shares, expects to top SpaceX's $75B IPO and courts Nvidia as a $10B anchor at a ~$2T valuation, Stripe buys the token router OpenRouter for >$7B, Meta rents trillions of Azure tokens a week, OpenAI cuts Sol pricing 20%, the SEC subpoenas Situational Awareness's lenders, a16z raises a $1.1B Machine Age Fund, Nvidia's quarter doubles to $96.2B, and Berkshire buys $10B of Alphabet's raise at a discount. ### (e) Open vs closed — "the Commons Road"
**Description.** Whether AI artefacts are shared or withheld has oscillated with each era. Academic openness (Weka 1993, Torch, Theano, Caffe 2014) gave way to corporate-but-open frameworks (TensorFlow 2015, PyTorch 2016–17, Hugging Face Transformers 2019) that made deep learning a commons. OpenAI's staged GPT-2 release (2019) was the first deliberate withholding on safety grounds; GPT-3 (2020) shipped as an API only, and GPT-4 (2023) without architectural details. The counter-movement — EleutherAI (2020), BLOOM (2022), Stable Diffusion (Aug 2022) — became mainstream when Meta's LLaMA weights leaked (3 Mar 2023) and Meta then embraced open weights with Llama 2 (Jul 2023) and Llama 3 (Apr 2024); Mistral (2023), Qwen and DeepSeek (V3 Dec 2024, R1 Jan 2025) extended the commons to Europe and China; even OpenAI returned with gpt-oss (Aug 2025). The Open Source Initiative's 2024 Open Source AI Definition formalised the "open weights ≠ open source" debate; in Aug 2026 the commons itself consolidates as Nvidia buys Hugging Face and SpaceX buys Cursor, while its centre of gravity is measured to be Chinese (Hugging Face's summer report; GLM-5.3 and Kimi K3 within a point of the proprietary frontier; ByteDance's 10T-parameter no-distillation bet) and Washington's Pax Silica letters turn open-vs-closed into a choice of bloc — even as Apple ships a proprietary model in China beside Qwen and Slack Code puts five labs' agents in one channel; the US replies at the small end (Meta's 30B Muse Glimmer, Nvidia's Nemotron 3.5 Lightning, Gemma's billionth download) and, per WSJ, with Nvidia's ~$6B trillion-parameter Nemotron 4, while China prices the commons at a tenth of the frontier (GLM-5.3-Flash at $0.15/$0.50, Qwen3.8-Flash, DeepSeek V4.1-Flash) and seeks US cloud distribution for Kimi K3 — until a joint NSA/CISA/FBI advisory names six Chinese labs for "industrial-scale" distillation. The commons also changes shape as a product: OpenClaw 2.0 ships from 933 contributors and 16,000 pull requests as a multiplayer agent workspace, Perplexity's Portable Computer runs a full agent stack locally on a DGX Spark with no per-token charge, Alibaba ships its Wan3.0 video model with weights "firmly shut" a week before open-weighting Qwen3.8-Flash, Meta's Muse Spark 1.3 undercuts the frontier on price while withholding both its max tier and its weights, and Cognition builds a proprietary frontier coding model on top of open-weights Kimi K3. Underneath, the commons is measured and sourced: Cloudflare dates the moment machine traffic passed human traffic to May 2026 and forecasts a 1,000× ratio, Zuckerberg's "The Future is for Everyone" makes billions of personal superintelligences the safety argument for open weights, Google buys Spirit Airlines' data estate out of bankruptcy for $10M to train on, and Japan adopts a "comply or explain" code asking labs to disclose their training data or say why not. ### (f) Natural language processing lineage — "the Language River"
**Description.** NLP is the thread that most directly leads to today's LLMs. It begins with the Georgetown–IBM machine-translation demo (1954) and the optimism ALPAC crushed in 1966; runs through symbolic dialogue systems (ELIZA 1964–67, SHRDLU 1968–70); turns statistical with IBM's speech and translation work (Jelinek from 1972; IBM alignment models, early 1990s) and hidden Markov models; becomes neural with word2vec (2013), seq2seq and attention (2014), Google Neural Machine Translation (2016) and LSTMs; and then floods into the Transformer plateau — ELMo, GPT, BERT (2018), GPT-3 (2020) — and the Generative Coast (ChatGPT 2022). ### (g) Computer vision lineage — "the Vision River"
**Description.** Vision starts in neuroscience — Hubel and Wiesel's 1959 cat-cortex recordings of orientation-selective cells — and in Larry Roberts's 1963 MIT thesis "Machine Perception of Three-Dimensional Solids"; MIT's 1966 "Summer Vision Project" famously underestimated the problem. Fukushima's Neocognitron (1979) and LeCun's LeNet convolutional networks (1989–1998) laid the neural groundwork while hand-engineered features (SIFT 1999, Viola–Jones 2001) dominated the 2000s. ImageNet (2009) and AlexNet's 2012 ILSVRC win flipped the field to deep learning; ResNet (2015), Faster R-CNN and YOLO (2015) made detection real-time; GANs (2014) began the generative turn; and the Vision Transformer (2020), CLIP (2021), diffusion models (Stable Diffusion 2022, Sora 2024) and Segment Anything (2023) merged vision into the multimodal foundation-model mainstream. ### (h) Game-playing milestones — "the Tournament Road"

The road passes IBM's landmark spectacles — Deep Blue beating Kasparov (May 1997) and Watson winning *Jeopardy!* (Feb 2011) — and Chinook, the first program to win a human world championship (checkers, 1994; game solved 2007). ## Schools of thought / sub-clusters
- **Rivers (technical lineages)** — RL (a), hardware (d), NLP (f), vision (g): drawn as watercourses that widen as they approach the Generative Coast, where (a), (f) and (g) merge into one delta (LLMs + RLHF + multimodality). borders: each rises in a specific western era (cybernetics, symbolic) and touches every continent en route; (a) borders (h) wherever self-play appears, (d) borders (g) at AlexNet.
- **Roads (social/institutional debates)** — safety/ethics (c) and open-vs-closed (e): drawn as roads with milestones and toll-gates crossing rivers via bridges (e.g. the GPT-2 staged release is a bridge where (c) and (e) cross). borders: paradigm-agnostic; they connect symbolic-era critics (Weizenbaum) to connectionist-era critics (Gebru/Bender), and (e) borders (d) at export controls.
- **Applied frontiers** — robotics (b) and game-playing (h): drawn as roads that alternate between banks because each era used them as demonstrations of its paradigm (STRIPS vs subsumption; minimax vs self-play); borders: (b) borders (g) at VLA models and (a) at policy-gradient control; (h) borders (a) from TD-Gammon onward and (d) at Deep Blue's custom chips.
